# RAG benchmarks

gosidian can answer questions on a project's documents: pipelines convert
the files and cut them into chunks, the database plugin stores and searches
the chunks, a reranker reorders them and a local model writes the answer,
which the chat plugin serves ([Bots](chat.md), [Pipelines](pipelines.md),
[Plugins](plugins.md)). Every default in that chain was picked with a
benchmark in the maintainers' lab. This page gives the results, with the
configuration and the hardware that produced them, so that whoever installs
gosidian knows what to expect, on what hardware, and why the defaults are
what they are.

It has two parts:

1. **Reference results** — the configuration in use, measured from the
   question to the judged answer, on two private corpora.
2. **The benchmarks behind the choices** — one per step of the pipeline:
   the alternatives tried, the numbers, the choice and its date. A benchmark
   stays here when a later one changes the choice.

The data is in [`bench/results/`](../bench/results/), one `rag-*.json` file
per benchmark or configuration: versions, hardware, settings, numbers and
date. The tables below are written from those files by
`python3 bench/rag/render.py`; a table edited by hand is overwritten at the
next run.

## Read this first

- **The judges are models, not people.** Answers are scored by two local
  models, once by a hosted one as well; the image descriptions were judged,
  and the reference pages of the conversion benchmark transcribed, by
  Claude. Judges have noise and preferences of their own. Where it could be
  checked they agree with each other, but agreement is not correctness.
- **The numbers hold for that corpus and those questions.** Another corpus,
  other questions, other users would move them. Read them for the order of
  magnitude and for the direction of a choice, not as a promise.
- **The corpora are private.** They belong to people who use gosidian and
  have not given permission to publish them, so they are described only by
  statistics: no names, no file names, no example of a question or an
  answer. Pages carry neutral codes (B-07, C-03).
- **One run per configuration.** On 120 questions, a tenth or two of a
  point, or a couple of percentage points, is within the noise (2-4
  questions).
- **One consumer GPU.** Every model runs on a 16 GB card. A larger card
  runs the same models faster and opens larger ones; the choices made to
  fit in 16 GB are marked as such.

A precise question asks for a fact that sits in one passage; a broad one
needs a part of a document, several paragraphs or sections.

### How answers are judged

A judge reads the question, the expected answer, the bot's answer and the
sources the bot was given (up to 18,000 characters), and gives a score from
−10 to +10 with a reason. Three rules: "I don't know" scores 0; what the
bot takes from its sources is not invented; leaving something out is not an
error. An answer is **good** at 7 or more and **wrong** below 0; the score
of a configuration is the mean of the judges. The judges are `gemma4:12b`
and `qwen3:14b` (Ollama, Q4_K_M, 12,288 tokens of context).

A first version of the judge saw only the expected passage: it gave −5 to
−10 to an honest "I don't know", counted as invented what the bot took from
another source and punished incomplete broad answers as wrong. It was
replaced before the comparisons below.

### How search is measured

- **doc@k** — share of questions whose document is among the first *k*
  chunks; **sec@k** — the same for the question's section (the section
  itself, one of its paragraphs or a subsection);
- **MRR** — mean of 1/rank of the first right document or section;
- **recall** — recall@10 of the index (HNSW) against the exact search of
  the same collection: what the index loses;
- **ms** — milliseconds of the store per question; embedding the question
  adds 40-70 ms.

- **Language and revision**: the bot reads the language of 97% of the
  questions and the revision asked for in all of them. In German, which

  Croatian is also where the two judges are farthest apart (3.2 points

### Document converter (2026-10-17)

**Choice: docling 0.5.0 stays the default converter, now with `office:

by Claude Opus from the page image, under fixed rules: headings at their
levels, tables in markdown (HTML when cells are merged), a placeholder
where each image sits, page headers and footers left out. Five of them are
being checked by hand; until then the reference is a model's reading. The
image descriptions are not part of this benchmark: they are a later step
of the pipeline, the same for every converter.

Values from 0 to 1, 1 meaning as the reference; there is no overall score,
the winner is picked format by format, as a pipeline can. *text*: 1 −
normalized character edit distance; *words*: F1 on the words; *headings*:
F1 of the headings; *levels*: matched headings at the right level;
*order*: blocks in the reference's order; *tables*: F1 on the cells;
*structure*: tables with the right rows and columns; *images*: their
number, and their position after the right block; *clean*: page headers
and footers removed. "(original file)": the tool read the Word, PowerPoint
or Excel file instead of the PDF, measured on the stretch of the document
that matches the page; Claude converted the whole manuals page by page.

  again to answer finds the parents of the paragraphs.
- **An icon needs what it looks like.** In a table of symbols each
  pictogram is an icon beside its meaning, and its label, written by the
  model that described it, says that meaning again. A question that
  describes the symbol by sight finds none of its own words there: with
  the labels alone the model answered "not found" to 5 of the 12, against
  2 without descriptions. With the first sentence of the description
  after the label, the first time the icon is in the document, it
  answered all 12: 9.96. An icon repeated beside every warning keeps its
  label alone after the first time, so its description does not weigh on
  every paragraph that shows it. On the 277 questions the icons described
  cost nothing (7.86 against 7.85). German drops from 8.65 to 7.00 on two
  questions of ten: one is a miss of the search, a neighbouring
  procedure found instead of the one asked (8.5 to −5.0), with no
  described icon among its sources; the other is wrong with either rule (−1.0 and
  −3.0).
- **Measured on scope C only.** The wiki of scope A keeps its images as
  paragraphs of their own and its icons with their labels, as measured
  there, until the two rules are measured on it.
- The 277 questions come from the text: they measure what the
  descriptions cost. The 20 on the figures measure what they bring, on a
  small sample: one question from 0 to 10 moves the mean by half a point.

## What comes next

- **Scope C in its other languages and families**: no question asks
  about the Chinese manual of the family measured here, and the
  collection holds Arabic, Russian, Greek and Korean manuals not yet
  imported.
- **A public corpus.** The same pipeline from question to judged answer on
  ViDoRe V3, a public collection of professional documents with figures
  and tables and questions checked by hand, in all its languages: a
  reference anyone can rerun.
- **Other people's results.** Once the JSON format of `bench/results/` is
  stable, a place for the numbers of other configurations, hardware and
  pipelines.

## Limits

- Private corpora, three of them with answers: the numbers cannot be
  checked from outside until the public corpus is in.
- Generated questions: the questions of scope A were written by Qwen3
  models from the documents, the family of the embedding model: a possible
  advantage to keep in mind.
- The questions of scopes B and C were written by Claude from the
  documents, the questions on the figures of scope C by the same Claude
  that set up the benchmark; the answers and the judges are local models.
- The local judges agree less in Croatian and Slovenian than in the other
  languages: over the five runs of scope C a mean gap of 2.0-3.2 points
  in Croatian and 1.6-2.4 in Slovenian, 0.5-1.8 in the others. The
  scores of those two languages are the least sure.
- One run per configuration, one card, one answer model family at a time.
- The conversion reference is a model's transcription, partly circular for
  the Claude rows; the Word figures use an independent quasi-reference.
